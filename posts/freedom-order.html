Freedom Order — An Executive Order

Issued by Queen Arun Sandhya

Looking back to July 2025, here is what Her Majesty set in motion.

1. Background: What Is an Executive Order?

A Monarch can provide input into policy and law in several ways:

  • Acts with Royal Assent
  • Executive Orders

This document contains details of an Executive Order. This has been issued to all subjects of the Monarch.

2. Purpose: Why a New Ethos?

There has been child slavery and prostitution affecting people who cannot find work, conducted through hidden but legal transactions.

I, Arun Sandhya as Monarch, say that we can do better than that. We can avoid child slavery altogether if we all watch out for one another. We can give our children a childhood free of slavery, extortion, and sexual exploitation.

The new ethos: Heart and soul, our citizens thrive loyally with freedoms. Queen Arun Sandhya, the Monarch, is defender of the free, and encourages subjects to meet their capacity for growth and ownership within the principles of this ethos and the law.

3. Directives for Company CEOs

  1. Restate your ethos explicitly. As an operating requirement of trading on the London Stock Exchange and of operating as a company in the UK, companies must ensure that their CEOs state their ethos: to avoid child labour, slavery, and extortion, and to ensure no legal transactions take place in support of these.
  2. Review your operating controls. CEOs may take a week to review their controls and ensure child slavery is not enshrined in their policies or technology. Until the review is complete, their status on the companies register will be "alert", visible on the stock exchange and reflected in their reputation. People can police themselves and report any issues.
  3. Pause automated interest calculations. Automated interest calculations have been identified as one example of the enshrinement of slavery. They have been paused, and the links between the accounts removed.

Consequences:

  • Company shares will be taken off trading on the FTSE until the company has published a statement on its website regarding its ethos, its commitment to the people of the country, and its commitment to avoiding slavery and extortion.
  • Any past involvement in slavery or extortion will form part of the company's and CEO's reputation on the Companies Register; people can decide for themselves whether to deal with them.
  • In due course, CEOs will undergo an operational review and must restate their commitment to the Monarch's and country's ethos, and meet operating licence requirements every year.

4. The Legal Framework in Transition

As such, this Executive Order is provided in addition to the Constitution. The laws and operating licences of the United Kingdom are changing as follows:

Unchanged for now:

  • Company law
  • Property law
  • Communications Act
  • Policing Act
  • Children's Act
  • UK emergency services — carry on operating until further notice

Under review:

  • Every other law and Act is under review with the Law Society.
  • Operating licences for all Local Authorities need to be reviewed.

5. Obligations of the People

  • Check your own affairs. People will check their own property and their services.
  • State your commitment. The people can express their commitment to the Monarch's ethos by defending their own children and property, and ensuring there is no perpetuation of slavery.
  • Report concerns. If you are in doubt about whether slavery or extortion is occurring, report the private company concerned through your local policing website to have its operating licence approval halted, until you have secured your own property and families.
